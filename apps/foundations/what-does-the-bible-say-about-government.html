What Does the Bible Say About Government?

Foundational Facts on Law, Order, Politics, and Our Constitutional Republic — Directly from the Infallible Word of God

By Rowley and Patty Shaw (Mt. Sinai LLC) • Attributing Nick Freitas, Ken Ham, Patricia Engler, Dr. Nathaniel Jeanson & Answers in Genesis (AiG), and Hillsdale College, with Gemini

Introduction: Why Christians Must Study Government from Scripture Alone

Modern Christians have been fed two opposite lies about government and politics. The first lie says that "nice" Christians should stay completely out of politics, blindly obey whatever the government commands, and surrender the public square to secular humanism. The second lie says that a political party, an election, or a human ruler can save a morally bankrupt nation without repentance before Almighty God. Both lies collapse the moment we open the Word of God (2 Timothy 3:16–17; Psalm 146:3).

God Himself instituted civil government, established the boundaries of law and order, ordained national borders, and commands His people to expose darkness while loving their neighbors (Genesis 9:5–6; Acts 17:26; Ephesians 5:11). Because the Bible is written by the one eternal Creator who cannot lie (Numbers 23:19; Titus 1:2; Hebrews 6:18), it is our sole, supreme authority (Sola Scriptura) for judging every law, constitution, court ruling, and political platform on earth.

Separating Wicked Ideologies from Deceived People

Throughout this book, we will examine the history and platforms of political movements—from the Tower of Babel to modern Socialism, Communism, Eugenics, and American political parties—under the uncompromising spotlight of Scripture. As we do, we must maintain a vital biblical distinction: we wage ruthless spiritual warfare against demonic ideologies and wicked platforms, while extending truth, prayer, and gospel compassion to deceived individuals (2 Corinthians 10:4–5; Ephesians 6:12). Every political party contains fallen sinners, compromised opportunists, and voters who have been misled by generations of propaganda; our standard is never a party label, but the immutable Word of God (Isaiah 8:20; 2 Timothy 2:24–26; Jude 1:22–23).

Standing on the Shoulders of Faithful Stewardship

In compiling this biblical and constitutional guide, we gratefully acknowledge the tireless work of modern defenders of biblical authority, constitutional originalism, and Christian civic stewardship—including Answers in Genesis (Ken Ham and staff) for defending the literal Genesis foundation of all moral law, Hillsdale College for preserving the primary-source history of the American Founding and the U.S. Constitution, and Christian statesmen and apologists like Nick Freitas for articulating why self-governance, righteous capability, and familial responsibility are inseparable from liberty. Yet even the finest human teachers are tested by one standard alone: "To the law and to the testimony! If they do not speak according to this word, it is because they have no dawn" (Isaiah 8:20).

Chapter 1: The Origin & Sword of Civil Authority

The God of Heaven's Armies & the Noahic Mandate

1.1 Before the Flood vs. After the Flood: Why God Instituted the Sword

To understand why human government exists, we must return to the literal history of Genesis. Between the Fall of Adam and the global Flood of Noah's day (~1,656 years), there was no formal institution of human civil government bearing the sword to execute murderers. When Cain murdered his brother Abel, God personally pronounced judgment but prohibited other men from executing Cain (Genesis 4:8–15). Unrestrained by civil execution, human depravity spiraled until "the earth was corrupt in the sight of God, and the earth was filled with violence (chamas, H2555)" and every intent of the thoughts of man's heart was only evil continually (Genesis 6:5, 6:11–13).

After judging the entire world with a catastrophic global Flood and preserving eight souls aboard the Ark (Genesis 7:17–24; 1 Peter 3:20; 2 Peter 2:5), God stepped out onto a cleansed earth and established the Noahic Covenant with all mankind. At the very center of that covenant, God instituted human civil government with one foundational command:

> "Surely I will require your lifeblood; from the hand of every beast I will require it. And from the hand of man, from the hand of every man's brother, I will require the life of man. Whoever sheds man's blood, by man his blood shall be shed, for in the image of God He made man." (Genesis 9:5–6)

Notice the exact Sola Scriptura logic of Genesis 9:6:

  1. The Foundation is Creation: Murder is the ultimate crime not because the State owns citizens, but because every human being—from conception in the womb to natural death—bears the literal image of God (tselem Elohim, H6754 / H430; Genesis 1:26–27; Psalm 139:13–16; James 3:6–9).
  2. The Delegation of Authority: For the first time in history, God commanded "by man his blood shall be shed"—delegating the physical sword of capital judgment to human authority to restrain evil and protect innocent blood from polluting the land (Numbers 35:31–33; Matthew 26:52; Acts 25:10-11).

1.2 YHWH Sabaoth: The God of Heaven's Armies and the Biblical Meaning of "The Sword"

Modern culture—and much of compromised modern Christendom—has invented a false, effeminate caricature of God that reduces His character to permissive tolerance while stripping away His holiness, wrath, and martial majesty. Scripture reveals the exact opposite. Yes, "God is love" (1 John 4:8) and the "God of peace" (Romans 15:33; Hebrews 13:20), yet over 280 times in the Old Testament—and echoed directly in the New Testament—He reveals Himself by His covenantal military name: YHWH Sabaoth (יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת, H3068 / H6635) — the LORD of Hosts, the God of Heaven's Armies (1 Samuel 17:45; Psalm 24:10; Isaiah 6:3; James 5:4; Revelation 19:14).

The pagan Roman god Mars is a pathetic myth compared to the living God of Scripture:

  • The LORD is a Warrior: At the Red Sea, Moses and Israel sang, "The LORD is a warrior (ish milchamah, H376 / H4421); the LORD is His name" (Exodus 15:3; Isaiah 42:13).
  • The Sword (chereb, H2719 / machaira, G3162) Means Lethal Force and War: In modern conversation, people speak of "the sword" as a polite metaphor. In the Bible, the sword almost universally denotes lethal judicial execution internally and organized warfare externally. When God declares, "I will bring a sword upon you" (Leviticus 26:25; Ezekiel 14:17), He is speaking explicitly of war—real armies, real bloodshed, and national conquest used as His righteous instrument to judge sin and discipline the very children He loves (Deuteronomy 32:41–42; Judges 2:14–15; Jeremiah 25:29; Hebrews 12:5–11).
  • Christ Returns with the Sword: Jesus Christ did not abolish the martial reality of God's justice. In His first coming, He willingly laid down His life as the Lamb of God to purchase our redemption (Isaiah 53:7; John 1:29; John 10:18); in His second coming, He returns on a white horse at the head of the armies of heaven, judging and waging war in righteousness, clothed in a robe dipped in blood, striking the nations with the sharp sword (rhomphaia, G4501) so that He Himself treads the winepress of the fierce wrath of God, the Almighty (Psalm 2:8–9; Isaiah 63:1–6; Matthew 10:34; Revelation 19:11–16).

A man or a nation cannot truly understand peace until they understand the Sword. True biblical peace (shalom, H7965) is never the cowardice of surrendering to evil; it is the righteous order preserved when wicked men know that the Sword of justice is sharp, lawful, and ready (1 Kings 4:24–25; Proverbs 20:26; Luke 11:21).

The Heart of the Warrior-King: The Watchman Mandate & the Call to Repentance (Ezekiel 33:1–19)

Yet why does the LORD of Hosts brandish His sword and warn nations of impending judgment? Never out of sadistic cruelty, and never because He delights in destroying sinners. Even in the Old Testament, right alongside the strict requirement of the Law, God swears by His own eternal life (chay-ani, H2416) in Ezekiel 33:11 that He takes zero pleasure in the death of the wicked, but earnestly desires that the wicked turn from his evil way and live (cf. 2 Peter 3:9; 1 Timothy 2:4).

In Ezekiel 33:1–9, God establishes the Watchman Mandate (tsaphah, H6822): when the LORD brings a sword upon a land, the watchman who sees the sword coming is commanded to blow the trumpet and warn the people. If the watchman stays silent out of cowardice or "niceness" and fails to warn the wicked to turn from his way, the wicked man will die in his iniquity, but God will require his blood at the watchman's hand (Ezekiel 33:6, 33:8; echoed by Paul in Acts 20:26–27). God's warnings of judgment—and the discipline of His law—are acts of fierce, fatherly love designed to wake sinners up before it is too late (Hebrews 12:5–11; Revelation 3:19).

1.3 God's Deacon of Wrath (diakonos & exousia in Romans 13)

In Romans 13:1–4 and 1 Peter 2:13–14, the Apostle Paul and the Apostle Peter establish the New Testament charter for civil government:

> "Every person is to be in subjection to the governing authorities (exousia, G1849). For there is no authority except from God, and those which exist are established by God... For rulers are not a cause of fear for good behavior, but for evil. Do you want to have no fear of authority? Do what is good and you will have praise from the same; for it is a minister (diakonos, G1249) of God to you for good. But if you do what is evil, be afraid; for it does not bear the sword (machaira, G3162) for nothing; for it is a minister of God, an avenger (ekdikos, G1558) who brings wrath (orge, G3709) on the one who practices evil." (Romans 13:1, 13:3–4)

Every word in this passage carries massive theological weight:

  • Delegated Authority (exousia, G1849): No president, king, congress, or judge possesses intrinsic authority. Their authority is strictly delegated from God's throne (John 19:11; Daniel 2:21). When a ruler acts outside God's charter—punishing the righteous and praising the wicked—he is acting as a rogue servant who will answer directly to the King of Kings (Psalm 2:10–12; Proverbs 17:15; Wisdom echoed in Isaiah 5:20).
  • God's Deacon (diakonos, G1249): The Greek word translated "minister" or "servant" in Romans 13:4 is diakonos—the exact same root word used for a church deacon (1 Timothy 3:8). Just as a church deacon serves the table of mercy inside the congregation, the civil magistrate is God's deacon of wrath in the public square, commissioned to protect the innocent and execute vengeance (ekdikos, G1558) upon evildoers (1 Peter 2:14).
  • Why Personal Vengeance vs. Civil Execution Differ: In Romans 12:19, Paul commands individual Christians, "Never take your own revenge, beloved, but leave room for the wrath of God." Four verses later, in Romans 13:4, he explains how God executes that earthly wrath against violent criminals: through the civil magistrate bearing the machaira (the lethal short sword of execution and defense). Refusing to punish violent murderers and predators is not "Christian compassion"—it is a direct violation of Romans 13:4 and Genesis 9:6 that brings bloodguilt upon the nation (Deuteronomy 19:10–13; Proverbs 24:11–12; Ecclesiastes 8:11).

1.4 Why Anarchy Is Rebellion Against God

Because governments frequently abuse their power, some well-meaning people swing to the opposite extreme and advocate anarchy—the abolition of all civil authority and law enforcement. Scripture condemns anarchy as a work of the flesh and a direct rebellion against God's created order (2 Peter 2:9–10; Jude 1:8).

Four times in the book of Judges, the Holy Spirit diagnoses the root cause of Israel's descent into unthinkable savagery, gang violence, and civil war:

> "In those days there was no king in Israel; every man did what was right in his own eyes." (Judges 17:6; Judges 21:25; cf. Judges 18:1; Judges 19:1)

Because the heart of fallen man is "more deceitful than all else and is desperately sick" (Jeremiah 17:9; Romans 3:10–18), removing the external restraint of lawful civil government never produces a peaceful utopia. Instead, anarchy creates an immediate power vacuum where the most ruthless warlords, gangs, and tyrants enslave the weak (Habakkuk 1:3–4; 1 Timothy 1:9–10). A tiny, strictly limited civil government that faithfully bears the sword against murder, theft, force, and fraud is not the enemy of human freedom—it is the God-ordained shield that preserves external order so families and churches can live "a tranquil and quiet life in all godliness and dignity" (1 Timothy 2:1–2).

Chapter 2: The Three Divine Jurisdictions

Family, Church & State

2.1 Jurisdiction Defined: Who Holds the Keys, the Rod, and the Sword?

Because God is the Creator and Supreme Ruler of heaven and earth (Genesis 1:1; Psalm 24:1; 1 Chronicles 29:11–12), all legitimate authority on earth flows from His throne and operates strictly within the boundaries He has drawn. In Scripture, God divides human authority into three distinct, covenantal jurisdictions: the Family, the Church, and the State.

Each jurisdiction has a God-ordained head, a specific sphere of responsibility, and a distinct instrument of correction:

  • The Family is entrusted with the Rod of Instruction and Discipline (shebet, H7626 / paideia, G3809) to nurture, educate, and train children in the fear of the LORD (Deuteronomy 6:6–7; Proverbs 13:24; Proverbs 22:15; Ephesians 6:1–4).
  • The Church is entrusted with the Keys of the Kingdom and the Word of God (kleis, G2807 / logos, G3056) to proclaim the gospel, administer the ordinances, shepherd souls, and exercise spiritual discipline within the congregation (Matthew 16:18–19; Matthew 18:15–18; 1 Corinthians 5:11–13; Hebrews 13:17).
  • The State is entrusted with the Sword of Civil Justice (chereb, H2719 / machaira, G3162) to punish external crimes of violence, theft, and fraud while defending the nation from foreign aggression (Genesis 9:5–6; Romans 13:1–4; 1 Peter 2:13–14).

Tyranny begins the exact moment one jurisdiction invades another. When the Church attempts to wield the physical sword of the State, it produces corrupt religious inquisitions; when the State seizes the Rod of the Family or the Keys of the Church, it produces totalitarian statism.

2.2 The Family: The First and Foundational Government

Before God ever established a church, a king, or a civil court, He instituted the Family in the Garden of Eden (Genesis 1:27–28; Genesis 2:18–24; Matthew 19:4–6). Self-government inside the Christian home is the bedrock of all free societies: if men and women will not govern themselves and their households under God's Word, no number of police officers or constitutional parchment barriers can preserve liberty (1 Timothy 3:4–5; Titus 2:1–8).

Scripture is unmistakable regarding who owns and governs children:

  1. Children Are an Inheritance from YHWH, Not Creatures of the State: "Behold, children are a heritage (nachalah, H5159) of the LORD, the fruit of the womb is a reward" (Psalm 127:3–5). Notice to whom God delegates that stewardship: strictly to fathers and mothers, never to civil bureaucrats (Deuteronomy 6:4–9; Psalm 78:4–7; Proverbs 1:8; Ephesians 6:4).
  2. Marriage and Gender Are Fixed by the Creator: God created mankind male (zakar, H2145) and female (neqebah, H5347) and defined marriage as a lifelong covenant between one man and one woman (Genesis 1:27; Genesis 2:24; Hebrews 13:4). No legislature, president, or Supreme Court possesses the jurisdiction to redefine what the Creator established at the beginning (Isaiah 5:20; Romans 1:24–28).

2.3 The Church: Under the Sole Headship of Jesus Christ

Jesus Christ purchased the Church with His own blood (Acts 20:28) and reigns as her exclusive, supreme Head:

> "And He put all things in subjection under His feet, and gave Him as head (kephale, G2776) over all things to the church, which is His body, the fullness of Him who fills all in all." (Ephesians 1:22–23; cf. Colossians 1:18)

In the Old Testament, when King Uzziah grew proud and crossed the jurisdictional boundary between the throne and the altar—entering the temple of the LORD to burn incense on the altar of incense, a duty God assigned exclusively to the sons of Aaron—eighty courageous priests headed by Azariah withstood the king to his face:

> "It is not for you, Uzziah, to burn incense to the LORD, but for the priests, the sons of Aaron, who are consecrated to burn incense. Get out of the sanctuary, for you have been unfaithful and will have no honor from the LORD God." (2 Chronicles 26:18)

When Uzziah raged against the priests with the censer in his hand, the LORD struck the king directly on his forehead with leprosy until the day of his death (2 Chronicles 26:19–21). Civil rulers have zero authority to dictate church doctrine, silence the preaching of the Word, forbid corporate worship, or regulate the conscience of the bride of Christ (Daniel 6:10; Acts 4:18–20; Acts 5:28–29; Hebrews 10:24–25).

Remembering the Imprisoned Church & Bearing Christ's Reproach (Hebrews 13:3, 12–14, 23)

Closing out the New Testament epistle to the Hebrews—written to Jewish Christians wrestling with the transition from the Levitical priesthood to the finished work of Christ—Scripture shows us how the early Church lived out these three jurisdictions in the real world. On one hand, believers are commanded to honor the family covenant of marriage (Hebrews 13:4), stay free from the covetous love of money (Hebrews 13:5), and obey faithful spiritual shepherds who keep watch over their souls as those who will give an account to God (Hebrews 13:17).

On the other hand, when the civil State overstepped its jurisdiction and persecuted the Church for proclaiming Jesus as Lord above Caesar, believers did not bow. They went to Jesus "outside the camp (exo tes paremboles), bearing His reproach—for here we do not have a lasting city, but we are seeking the city which is to come" (Hebrews 13:13–14). They actively remembered fellow believers in chains (desmios, G1198) as though bound in prison with them (Hebrews 13:3), and rejoiced at the closing historical note in Hebrews 13:23 that their brother Timothy had just been released (apolyo, G630) from prison! Honoring God's institution of government never meant surrendering the freedom of the Gospel to the State.

2.4 The State: A Strictly Limited Shield, Never a Provider of Charity or Welfare

According to both the Word of God and the original U.S. Constitution, practical, physical welfare—caring for the poor, the meek, the widow, the orphan, and the infirm—was never intended to be the responsibility of the civil State. That sacred responsibility falls squarely upon the shoulders of the Family and Christ's Church.

Look at the explicit jurisdictional hierarchy God established in Scripture for caring for those in need:

  1. First Line of Compassion — The Family (1 Timothy 5:3–8, 16): Before the Church or anyone else is burdened, children and grandchildren are commanded to care for their own widowed mothers, aging parents, and infirm relatives to repay their parents, because if anyone does not provide for his own—and especially for those of his household—he has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever (1 Timothy 5:4, 5:8, 5:16; cf. Mark 7:9–13).
  2. Second Line of Compassion — The Church & Individual Believers (James 1:27; Acts 6:1–6): When a widow, orphan, or disabled neighbor has no family able to help, the responsibility passes directly to the Bride of Christ. Pure and undefiled religion in the sight of our God and Father is to visit orphans and widows in their distress and to keep oneself unstained by the world (James 1:27; cf. Deuteronomy 14:28–29; Deuteronomy 15:7–11; Isaiah 58:6–10; Matthew 25:35–40; Galatians 2:10). Indeed, the very office of church deacon (diakonos, Acts 6:1–6; 1 Timothy 3:8–13) was created specifically so the local congregation could administer daily physical food and care to true widows with discernment, personal love, and spiritual accountability!
  3. Why the State Has Zero Business Running Welfare: A cold, godless bureaucracy cannot love anyone. Only people indwelt by the grace and love of Jesus Christ can truly care for God's children—ministering to both body and soul while maintaining biblical accountability: comforting the truly disabled and widowed (1 Timothy 5:3, 5:9–10), while requiring able-bodied men and women to work with their own hands just as God's gleaning laws required the poor to harvest the corners of the field with dignity (Leviticus 19:9–10; Leviticus 23:22; Ruth 2:2–3; 2 Thessalonians 3:10–12).

Tragically, modern society—and a lazy, compromised Church—has pushed the ministry of mercy off the shoulders of Christians and demanded that the government handle it. The government has no more business attempting to "love" and feed people through forced taxation than it has educating our children! Whenever the State steps outside Romans 13 to become the provider of bread, the educator of children, and the counterfeit savior of the poor, it steals from working families (Exodus 20:15), subsidizes fatherlessness and sloth, destroys the witness of the Church, and enslaves citizens to the throne of Caesar (1 Samuel 8:15–18; Psalm 2:1–3).

Chapter 3: What Is True Law?

God's Moral Law, American Law & the Bankruptcy of Atheism

3.1 The Atheist's Dilemma: Without the Lawgiver, Morality Is a Fiction

Every human law enforces a moral standard. Whenever a government passes a statute declaring, "You shall not murder," or "You shall not steal," it is making an absolute moral claim about right and wrong. Yet this confronts secular humanism and atheism with an inescapable dilemma: if there is no eternal Creator and Lawgiver, objective morality cannot exist.

Scripture diagnoses the root of atheism with surgical precision:

> "The fool (nabal, H5036) has said in his heart, 'There is no God.' They are corrupt, they have committed abominable deeds; there is no one who does good." (Psalm 14:1; cf. Psalm 53:1)

Consider the logical consequence of atheistic materialism. If the universe is nothing more than a cosmic accident of matter and energy in motion, and human beings are merely evolved bags of chemicals descending from apelike ancestors, then why is one bag of chemicals killing another bag of chemicals "evil"? In a godless universe, a murderer shooting his neighbor is no different morally than baking soda reacting with vinegar, or a lion killing a gazelle. Under atheism, there is no good, no evil, no human dignity, and no unalienable rights—only raw power, where the strong devour the weak.

Whenever an atheist demands "justice," "human rights," or "fairness," he has to steal moral capital from the biblical worldview while denying the God who created him. Objective moral law (torah, H8451 / nomos, G3551) exists only because the eternal, unchanging Creator wrote His moral standard both on tablets of stone at Mount Sinai and upon the human conscience:

> "For when Gentiles who do not have the Law do instinctively the things of the Law, these, not having the Law, are a law to themselves, in that they show the work of the Law written in their hearts, their conscience (syneidesis, G4893) bearing witness and their thoughts alternately accusing or else defending them." (Romans 2:14–15; cf. Isaiah 33:22; James 4:12)

3.2 Why the Founders Welcomed All Citizens Yet Required a Creator

When America's Founders framed the Declaration of Independence and the U.S. Constitution, they rejected a national state-run church so that no denomination could use federal troops to persecute another. Yet the Founders were equally explicit that a free system of law cannot survive on atheism.

The legal bedrock of the American Founding—studied by virtually every colonial lawyer and legislator—was Sir William Blackstone's Commentaries on the Laws of England (1765), which anchored all legitimate human law directly in the Revealed Word of God:

  • The Law of Nature and of Nature's God: Blackstone demonstrated that God's eternal moral law, revealed supremely in the Holy Scriptures, is the supreme standard: "Upon these two foundations, the law of nature and the law of revelation, depend all human laws; that is to say, no human laws should be suffered to contradict these" (cf. Psalm 19:7–9; Psalm 119:142, 119:160).
  • Why Oaths in Court Require the Supreme Judge: In a biblical and constitutional court of law, witnesses place their hand on the Bible and swear an oath ("So help me God") because human courts cannot see into a man's secret heart. Without the fear of an omniscient God who will judge every perjurer in eternity, an oath is meaningless breath (Exodus 20:7; Leviticus 19:12; Ecclesiastes 12:14; Hebrews 6:16).

3.3 Where Biblical Law and American Law Align—and Why They Rightly Differ

One of the greatest sources of confusion among both Christians and skeptics is the question: "If America's laws were built on the Bible, why doesn't the U.S. Constitution execute people for worshipping a false god or breaking the Sabbath?" Rightly dividing the Word of Truth (2 Timothy 2:15) gives us a crystal-clear, unassailable answer.

When God gave the Ten Commandments (asereth ha-debarim, Exodus 20:1–17; Deuteronomy 5:6–21), He divided them across Two Tables of Stone that Jesus Himself summarized as love for God and love for neighbor (Matthew 22:36–40; Romans 13:8–10):

  1. The First Table (Commandments 1–4: Duty Toward God): Having no other gods before YHWH, making no idols, not taking the LORD's name in vain, and keeping the Sabbath holy. Under the Old Covenant theocracy of national Israel—where God Himself dwelt physically in the Tabernacle/Temple as Israel's direct civil King and the land was holy covenant turf—violating the First Table was high treason punishable by the civil magistrate (Deuteronomy 13:1–11; Deuteronomy 17:2–7).
  2. The Second Table (Commandments 5–10: Duty Toward Man): Honoring father and mother, not murdering, not committing adultery/sexual violence, not stealing, not bearing false witness (perjury/fraud), and not coveting a neighbor's property.

In the New Covenant, Jesus Christ declared, "My kingdom is not of this world" (John 18:36), and commanded that the wheat and the tares grow together in the world until the final harvest, when He Himself will send His angels to judge unbelievers and idolaters in the lake of fire (Matthew 13:24–30, 13:36–43; 2 Corinthians 10:4; Revelation 21:8). No earthly nation today is national covenant Israel. Saving faith and true worship of Jesus Christ can never be produced at the point of a government bayonet. If God had wanted mindless robots programmed to bow on command, He could have spoken automatons into existence—or stationed cherubim with flaming swords around the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil so Adam and Eve could never touch it (compare Genesis 2:16–17 with Genesis 3:24). Instead, God created man in His own image with moral agency and commanded voluntary love and obedience: "Choose for yourselves today whom you will serve... but as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD" (Joshua 24:15; cf. Deuteronomy 30:19; Matthew 23:37; Revelation 22:17). When a civil government attempts to force First-Table worship at gunpoint, it creates hypocrites rather than converts, usurping a throne that belongs to God alone.

Therefore, the American Founders applied intense biblical wisdom:

  • American Criminal & Civil Law Directly Enforces the Second Table: Our laws against murder and assault (6th Commandment), rape and family abandonment (5th & 7th Commandments), theft, burglary, and embezzlement (8th Commandment), and courtroom perjury, slander, and contract fraud (9th Commandment) come straight out of Exodus 20–23 and Romans 13:8–10.

In Luke 19:13, the King commands His servants to occupy / do business (pragmateuomai, G4231) until He comes—raising godly children (Deuteronomy 6:6–7), proclaiming the Gospel without compromise (Matthew 28:18–20), defending innocent life (Proverbs 24:11–12), and standing unmoved in the full armor of God (Ephesians 6:10–18).

14.3 The Return of the King of Kings (Isaiah 9:6–7; Revelation 19:11–16)

Human history is not spiraling into random chaos, nor will globalist tyrants and the end-times Beast (Revelation 13) have the final word. History is marching straight toward the literal, physical, glorious return of Jesus Christ—the Lion of the Tribe of Judah and YHWH Sabaoth, the Commander of Heaven's Armies (Revelation 5:5; Revelation 19:11–16).

Look at how every tension in human government is permanently resolved when Jesus Christ takes His throne:

  1. Perfect Lawgiver, Judge, and King United in One Sinless Person (Isaiah 9:6–7; Isaiah 33:22): In Chapter 9, we saw why human government must divide power across three branches: because fallen men cannot be trusted with combined authority. When the Child born to us—the Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Eternal Father, Prince of Peace—governs with the government resting upon His shoulders, there will be no end to the increase of His government or of peace, establishing and upholding it with justice (mishpat) and righteousness (tsedaqah) forevermore (Isaiah 9:6–7; cf. Psalm 72:1–14; Jeremiah 23:5–6).
  2. Swords Beaten into Plowshares Under Righteous Rule (Isaiah 2:2–4; Micah 4:1–4): False human globalists at the United Nations carve Isaiah 2:4 onto stone walls while rejecting the Prince of Peace—trying to manufacture peace through disarmament and tyranny. True, lasting peace comes only when the Lord Himself judges between the nations from Jerusalem, enforces His Word with a rod of iron (Psalm 2:9; Revelation 19:15), and removes rebellion from the earth so that every man finally sits safely under his own vine and fig tree with no one to make him afraid (Micah 4:3–4).
  3. Every Knee Will Bow (Philippians 2:9–11; Revelation 20:11–15): Every president, prime minister, Supreme Court justice, dictator, voter, and citizen who ever lived will stand before the Great White Throne of Jesus Christ (2 Corinthians 5:10; Revelation 20:11–15). On that day, no political party label, no judicial robe, and no majority vote will excuse a single violation of God's Word—and every knee in heaven, on earth, and under the earth will bow and confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father (Philippians 2:10–11).
